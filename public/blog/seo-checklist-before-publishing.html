The SEO Checklist We Run Before Publishing Anything

Five groups of checks, five minutes, run before every page on this site goes live — including this one.

Published September 2, 2026 · Updated September 13, 2026 · 6 min read

This is the exact checklist we run through before publishing anything on this site, including this post. It's organized into five groups so you can move through it in a few minutes without missing the parts that matter most for rankings and reader trust.

1. Content and intent

  • The page clearly answers the searcher's main problem in the first two paragraphs.
  • The information is accurate and current enough for the topic — check anything time-sensitive.
  • The article adds a specific detail, example or number that generic advice on the same topic doesn't have.
  • Every claim a reader could act on has been checked against a reliable source.

2. Title, H1 and metadata

  • The title accurately describes the page — no promise the content doesn't keep.
  • The H1 is clear and matches the page topic (and usually matches the title closely).
  • The meta description reads like something a person would want to click, not a keyword list.
  • The URL is short, readable, and won't need to change later.

3. Structure and readability

  • Headings create a logical hierarchy — a reader skimming just the H2s should understand the whole page.
  • Paragraphs are short enough to scan comfortably on a phone screen.
  • Lists or tables are used only where they genuinely improve comprehension, not as filler.
  • Important information isn't hidden behind a tab, accordion or other interaction a crawler might not open.

4. Links and images

  • Internal links point to genuinely related pages, not just any page with matching keywords.
  • External links point to trustworthy, current sources where a claim needs backing.
  • Every link has been clicked once to confirm it goes where it says it goes.
  • Images have accurate alt text and are compressed for fast loading.

5. Technical checks

  • The page has a correct, self-referencing canonical URL.
  • It's indexable if you intend it to be indexed — check the robots meta tag.
  • Open Graph and Twitter card metadata are set so shared links look correct.
  • Structured data matches the visible content exactly and validates without errors.
  • The page renders correctly on mobile without relying on a fragile interaction to reveal the main content.
The final test before hitting publish
Read the page once as a first-time visitor who has never seen your target keyword.
If the page doesn't quickly make sense without knowing what you were optimizing for, fix the reading experience before worrying about any remaining optimization details.

After publishing

Submit the new URL through your normal Search Console workflow and check back after 2–3 weeks. Real query data often reveals a phrasing or subtopic you didn't plan for — that's usually a better source of your next update than guessing in advance.

Frequently asked questions

How long should this checklist take to run through?

For a typical blog post, 5–10 minutes once it becomes a habit. It's meant to be a final pass, not a redesign of the article.

Do I need structured data on every page?

Only where it's genuinely accurate and applicable — Article schema for blog posts, FAQPage schema when you actually have a Q&A section, and so on. Adding schema that doesn't match the visible content can do more harm than good.

What's the single most common thing this checklist catches?

Broken or mismatched internal links — pages that get renamed or removed while old posts still link to the original URL. It's worth checking even on pages you haven't touched in months.

Make it a habit, not a one-time audit

None of these checks are complicated on their own — the value comes from actually running through all of them, consistently, before every page goes live.

If you're generating a first draft with 30SecSEO, treat this checklist as the step between the generated blueprint and the published page — not an optional extra.
